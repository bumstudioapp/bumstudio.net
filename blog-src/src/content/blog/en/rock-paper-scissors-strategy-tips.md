People call rock paper scissors a coin flip. It isn't — not against humans. Studies of real
matches keep finding the same habits, and every habit is something you can exploit, especially in
RPS Master's five-round format where you get time to read your opponent.

## 1. Expect rock from beginners

Inexperienced players — and most players in their very first round — throw **rock** more than
anything else. Opening with paper is statistically the safest first move against a stranger.

## 2. Winners tend to repeat

After winning a round, players often throw the **same move again** ("if it worked, why change?").
Beat their last winning move and you steal the round they thought was safe.

## 3. Losers tend to switch — downwards

Players who lose frequently switch to the move that would have *lost to* the move they just
played, cycling rock → paper → scissors in their head. Track the direction of their switches
instead of just the last throw.

## 4. Scissors is the rarest throw

Across large samples, scissors is played least. That makes **rock quietly profitable** over long
sessions — it never loses to the most common "safe" answers as often as people fear, and it crushes
the throw nobody dares to skip twice.

## 5. Use draws as information

A draw means your opponent mirrored your thinking. Most players change after a draw — usually to
the move that beats the one just drawn. Play the move that beats *that*.

## 6. Best-of-five rewards patience

In RPS Master you don't need to win round 1 — you need three good reads in five rounds. Spend the
first two rounds testing: repeat a throw, watch the response, then strike with what you learned
in rounds 3–5.

## 7. Tilt is real — yours and theirs

After two straight losses, players throw more emotionally (lots of rock). If that's you, take the
rematch slowly and reset to your plan. If that's your opponent, keep paper ready.

## The takeaway

There is no unbeatable sequence — there is only paying attention. RPS Master rewards exactly
that: the Arena, ghost sequences in Conquest and rematches all give you repeated chances to read
one opponent deeply. Start with these seven habits and your win rate will climb faster than any
"random" strategy ever could.
